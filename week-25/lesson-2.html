Isolation and the Road to War

From Cash and Carry to Pearl Harbor

📖 TODAY'S LESSON

When Germany invaded Poland in September 1939, Roosevelt told the country that it could not expect him to stay neutral in thought, but he promised to keep it out of the fighting. He also asked Congress to change the Neutrality Actsnoo-TRAL-ih-tee aktsLaws passed in the 1930s that banned arms sales to countries at war and kept Americans out of war zones.. In November, Congress agreed. The new law allowed Britain and France to buy weapons from the United States, but on one condition. They had to pay in full and carry the goods away in their own ships. This policy was called cash and carrykash and KAIR-eeA policy that let nations at war buy goods in the United States only if they paid in full and carried the goods away in their own ships.. Its supporters claimed that it would keep Americans out of danger. Its real effect was to help the Allied PowersAL-yd POW-urzThe nations that fought the Axis in World War II, led by Britain, the Soviet Union, and the United States., the nations fighting Germany, because the British navy controlled the Atlantic.

The war then changed very quickly. In April 1940, Germany overran Denmark and Norway. In May, it struck Belgium, the Netherlands, and France. Within six weeks, France, which many people thought had the strongest army in Europe, had surrendered. Britain's army escaped from the beach at Dunkirk, but it left most of its heavy weapons behind. The British prime minister, Winston ChurchillWIN-stun CHUR-chilThe prime minister of Britain from 1940, who led his country through most of the war., had replaced Chamberlain on the day the attack began. He refused to make peace. That summer, German planes tried to destroy the Royal Air Force and then began bombing British cities. Britain stood almost alone. The long policy of appeasementuh-PEEZ-mentThe policy of giving in to an aggressor's demands in the hope of keeping the peace. had failed.

Americans were shocked. In September 1940, Congress passed the Selective Service Actsih-LEK-tiv SUR-vis aktThe 1940 law that created the first peacetime draft in American history., the first peacetime draft in American history. Men between twenty-one and thirty-five had to register. The same month, Roosevelt traded fifty old navy destroyers to Britain in return for the use of British bases in the Western Hemisphere. He did this on his own authority, without a vote of Congress, and some people said that he had gone too far.

That fall was also an election year. Since George Washington, no president had served more than two terms. In 1940, Roosevelt broke the custom and ran for a third. His opponent, Wendell Willkie, supported aid to Britain, too, so the voters were not given a plain choice. Roosevelt won. During the campaign, he promised the mothers and fathers of America that their sons would not be sent into any foreign wars. That promise would soon be tested.

By late 1940, Britain was running out of money to pay for weapons. Roosevelt had to find a new way to help. He offered a simple picture. If your neighbor's house is on fire, he said, you do not argue about the price of your garden hose. You lend it, and you get it back when the fire is out. The result was the Lend-Leaselend LEESA 1941 law that let the president lend or lease weapons and supplies to any country whose defense he thought vital to the United States. Act, passed in March 1941. It allowed the president to lend or lease weapons and supplies to any country whose defense he thought was vital to the United States. By the end of the war, the United States had sent over fifty billion dollars of goods to its allies. The country called itself neutral, but it was no longer acting like a neutral.

Not everyone agreed, and isolationismeye-suh-LAY-shun-iz-umThe belief that a country should stay out of the quarrels and wars of other nations. was still strong. The America First Committeeuh-MAIR-ih-kuh furst kuh-MIT-eeA group, active in 1940 and 1941, that worked to keep the United States out of the war., founded in 1940, became the largest group fighting against American entry into the war. It had hundreds of thousands of members. Its best-known speaker was the famous pilot Charles Lindbergh. Supporters of the group believed that America's defense depended on its own navy and air force, and that Britain could not be saved. They feared that Roosevelt was leading the country into war step by step. Some of Lindbergh's speeches also blamed Jewish Americans and the British for pushing the country toward war, and that hurt the group's good name. Historians still disagree about whether the isolationists were wrong about the danger or simply wrong about the answer.

In June 1941, the war widened again when Hitler invaded the Soviet Union, breaking his deal with Stalin. In August, Roosevelt and Churchill met on warships off Newfoundland and issued the Atlantic Charterat-LAN-tik CHAR-terA statement of war aims issued by Roosevelt and Churchill in August 1941. It promised no new territory, self-government for peoples, and freedom of the seas., a joint statement of the goals they hoped to reach after the war. It promised that neither would seek new territory, that peoples should choose their own governments, and that the seas should be free. Meanwhile, in the Atlantic, German submarines and American ships began to fight. In October, a German submarine sank the destroyer USS Reuben James, killing about a hundred sailors. There was no declaration of war, but there was an undeclared naval conflict.

In Asia, a different crisis was coming. Japan, which was already at war with China, joined Germany and Italy in 1940 in a pact. Together, the three became known as the Axis PowersAK-sis POW-urzGermany, Italy, and Japan, the nations that fought the Allies in World War II.. In the summer of 1941, Japan moved its troops into southern Indochina, which was then a French colony. Japan needed oil, rubber, and metal to keep fighting, and the colonies of Southeast Asia had them. Roosevelt answered with an embargoem-BAR-gohA government order that stops trade with another country., a government order that stops trade with another country. He froze Japanese money in American banks and cut off oil. Japan imported most of its oil from the United States. The leaders in Tokyo now faced a choice. They could give up their conquests in China, or they could take the oil of Southeast Asia by force. They chose force, and they decided that to do so they must first destroy the American fleet.

On the morning of Sunday, December 7, 1941, Japanese planes launched from six aircraft carriers attacked the naval base at Pearl Harborpurl HAR-burThe American naval base in Hawaii that Japan attacked on December 7, 1941, bringing the United States into the war., Hawaii. The attack sank or badly damaged eight battleships and destroyed about 188 aircraft. It killed 2,403 Americans. The aircraft carriers of the Pacific Fleet were at sea that day, which later proved very important. The American commanders were surprised. They had known that war with Japan was likely, but they had expected an attack in Southeast Asia.

The next day, Roosevelt asked Congress for a declaration of war. The Senate voted for it unanimously, and the House voted 388 to 1. Years of argument ended in a few hours. Three days later, Hitler declared war on the United States, though no treaty required him to do so. It was one of the most important choices of the war. If he had not, Americans might have focused much of their effort on Japan alone. Instead, the United States was now at war with all three Axis powers.

Could America have stayed out? Some say no, because the Axis nations were planning to dominate their regions, and a free republic could not live in a world run by dictators. Others say the oil embargo pushed Japan into a corner, and that a more cautious policy might have kept the peace for a time. As you read the sources this week, notice what each president and each critic saw at the time, not what we know now. Next week, the war begins in earnest, in Europe, where Americans were about to learn what the Nazi regime had actually done.
